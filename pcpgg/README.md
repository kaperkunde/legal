Legal documentation for **pcp.gg**, the bridge that gives the PCP on an owner's own computer a
public name at [pcp.gg](https://pcp.gg). pcp.gg is a trade name of Kaperkunde.

pcp.gg is free and invite-only during the alpha. There is **no acceptance gate**: these
documents are published rather than clicked through, and creating an account is what accepts
them; the sign-up form says so and links to them.

### Why pcp.gg has terms at all

pcp.gg only carries connections it cannot read, so it needs few rules, but not none. It hands out
names under `pcp.gg` and carries traffic for them, which makes it an intermediary service under
the EU Digital Services Act: the restrictions it puts on use (reserved names, taking a name back,
cutting a tunnel) have to be stated in its terms, and it has to name a point of contact. Dutch law
also only lets the "offered as it is" limit of liability bind when it is in terms made available
before the account is made. That is all the terms are for.

[PCP](../pcp/README.md) itself is open-source software with documents of its own; pcp.gg is a
separate, optional service.

## How these are published

pcp.gg renders these files directly. The app carries this repository as a git submodule at
`legal/` and renders the markdown on its pages, so `/terms`, `/privacy` and `/subprocessors` on
the site are this folder, and `/privacy/kaperkunde` is the company-wide policy beside them.
Changing a document here and bumping the submodule is what publishes it.

A change to what pcp.gg can see changes the [Privacy Notice](privacy.md) here and the site's
[security page](https://pcp.gg/security) together.
