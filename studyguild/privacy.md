## What is public, and what is not

This is the one thing to know before anything else. Study Guild is a competitive, public place,
and a guild is a shared deck on the open web.

**Public, readable by anyone, signed in or not:**

- A guild's name, subject and description, and every card in it: question, answer, pictures and
  links. Each card has a page of its own showing the card, its wordings credited to their
  authors' public names, its score with the up and down votes counted, how many members gave
  each kind of feedback on it (for instance that it seemed incorrect), and the duplicate
  decisions made about it. The page never names who gave feedback, voted or decided, and your
  own progress on a card is shown only to you.
- Your public name: the username you chose when you signed up. (Accounts made before usernames
  were required may still show a first name and the initial of the last name, "Kevin L."; set a
  username in Settings to change that.)
- Your member page, under your public name: your XP and level, your streak and best streak, the
  guilds you are in with your XP and level in each, your Contribution Points, how many cards you
  wrote and improved, the feats you earned and the month you joined.
- The leaderboards, which rank members by XP (all time and by week) and by Contribution Points,
  and each guild's own boards and top contributors; and the page a visitor lands on from your
  invite link, which says who invited them.

Guild pages are listed in the site's sitemap, described to search engines in their structured
data, summarised for AI crawlers at `/llms.txt`, and reachable by any assistant a member has
connected. Member pages ask search engines not to list them, but anyone who has the address can
open one. Once a card is in a guild it is published in that sense, and it stays published: the
[terms of use](terms.md#2-what-you-add-belongs-to-the-guild) say when a card becomes the
guild's rather than yours.

**Private, yours alone:** which cards you hold and how each one is doing for you (its rank and
due date), your training sessions and individual answers, the cards you hid, your votes and the
reports you made, your email address and full name, your time zone, and your settings.

So write into a guild only what you are happy to have read by strangers: no personal details,
yours or anybody else's, and nothing you need to keep private.

## What we hold

- **Your account.** The email address you signed up with, your name, a username if you chose
  one, and your password, stored hashed, so nobody at Kaperkunde can read it. Each sign-in
  session records the IP address and browser it was started from. Emailed sign-in links and
  password-reset links are kept until they expire or are used.
- **Your settings.** Your time zone, which your browser reports once so that "today" follows
  your clock; your daily goal; which email notifications are on and at what time; and the
  colour you chose for each guild.
- **What you add to guilds.** The guilds you founded; every card and every wording of a card
  you wrote, with any picture or link on it; banners you proposed for a guild; your votes; and
  a log of the cards you added next to a card that asked nearly the same thing, and whether you
  said yours asked something different. That log exists so we can review the duplicate check and
  tune it, not to judge you. The pictures you upload are counted, too, so that nobody can flood
  the site.
- **Your training.** Your stacks and their settings; each card's rank, due date and history in
  your stack; the cards you hid; every answer you give (which card, right or wrong, the rank it
  moved to, the XP, and your local day); your training sessions and their results; and the
  totals they add up to: Guild XP, level, streak, best streak, contributor score, the feats you
  earned, and the last day you trained or visited.
- **Reports.** When you mark a card as bad you give a reason; we keep who reported which card
  and why, and whether it has been handled and the reporter told, so we can deal with abuse.
  The reporter is never shown to anyone but us. Reports reach Kaperkunde's support mailbox as a
  digest of the report text and the card, without the reporter's email address.
- **Moderation.** If an account is suspended we keep that and the reason. Every moderation
  action (who acted, on which content, and why) is recorded, and the record stays after the
  affected account is deleted, with the link to the member cleared.
- **Your age.** That you confirmed you are 16 or over, and when. No date of birth.
- **Invitations.** Your invite link for each guild, and who joined through whose link. Both
  members can see that much: you see how many joined through yours, and the guild page tells a
  visitor who invited them.
- **Notifications.** For each kind of optional email, whether it is on, and when we last sent it
  to you, so you never get two; for streak notices, what was offered to you and whether you
  declined it. The last day you visited is kept so that we do not nag you when you have just
  been here.
- **Assistants you connect.** How the app described itself when it asked for access, what you
  allowed it to do and when, and the access tokens it holds, stored hashed, so a copy of our
  database would not let anyone act as you.
- **Ordinary server logs.** Requests to the site, with IP address and timestamp, kept briefly
  so we can fix things that break and stop people abusing the service.

That is the whole list. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixel and no
profiling of you anywhere in Study Guild.

### Cookies

Study Guild sets two kinds of cookie, both its own and neither used to track you:

- the cookie that keeps you signed in (and, for a moment, the ones a sign-in or an
  assistant's consent page needs);
- `sg_invite`, set only when you open a member's invite link. It holds the invite code and
  nothing about you. It lets us credit the member who invited you if you then join that guild,
  and is removed when you join or after thirty days, whichever comes first.

We ask for no consent because neither cookie is used for analytics or advertising and neither is
shared with anyone. If you do not want the invitation remembered, clear your cookies, or do not
open the link.

### Pictures on cards and banners

A picture you put on a card, or a banner you propose for a guild, is cropped and re-encoded before
it is stored, and every piece of metadata in the file (the camera, the date, the place it was
taken) is dropped in the process. What is kept is the picture itself, and it is public: anyone who
can see the card or the guild can see it. Once a picture is on a card it does not change; editing
the card points it at a new picture, and the old one stays with the earlier wording. A picture
uploaded for a card that was never saved is deleted after a day.

### Rate limits

To keep one runaway script from spoiling the site for everyone, we count requests to the
sign-in pages by IP address, and the sign-in and reset emails sent to one address, over the last
minute or hour. These counts live in memory and are gone as soon as the window passes; they are
never written down. How many cards and guilds you
may add in a day is worked out from the cards and guilds you have already added, so it needs
nothing more than that.

## Why we hold it

Your account, the guilds you are in and the cards you add are there because we cannot run the
service you asked for without them: that is the contract between us. Your training history
is the substance of that service: it is how a card knows its rank and when it is due. Your
votes and edits are shared work, and the guild needs to know who did what. The public boards
and member pages are part of the game you joined. The logs, the rate limits, the reports, the
duplicate log and the visit record rest on our legitimate interest in keeping the service up,
secure and worth using. An assistant you connect rests on your own choice to connect it. Nothing
here is decided about you by a machine, apart from refusing a card that duplicates another.

**Email.** We send two kinds. *Essential account mail* (the welcome, sign-in links, password
resets, and the explanation we owe you when we act on your content or account) goes with having
an account. *Optional study notifications*, such as training reminders, streak notices and a
single note after a month away, each have their own switch in Settings, and every one carries a
link that turns that kind off in one click. They pause on their own if you stay away. Settings
shows which are on, and what time they come at, in your own zone. We send no marketing mail.

## An assistant you connect

Study Guild can be added to Claude, or to any other assistant that speaks the Model Context
Protocol, as a connector. Doing so is your choice, made on a page that shows exactly what the
app asks for before anything is shared. Connecting an assistant is also subject to that
assistant's own terms, including its age rules. What it can ask for:

- to know which account you are, and to see your name and email address;
- to see guilds, cards and your own stacks;
- to stay connected without asking you to sign in again (it then holds a refresh token);
- only if you tick it, to act for you: add and improve cards, found guilds, upload pictures,
  vote, delete your own cards, and start or resume a study session and record your answers,
  which count towards your XP, streak and card ranks exactly as if you had given them.

The connection is a token held by that app, not a copy of your password, and you can take it
back at any time under Settings → Connected apps. Disconnecting takes effect on the app's very
next request.

What that assistant reads, it reads as you, and what it does with it is between you and whoever
makes it: it is your assistant, not ours, and not a Kaperkunde subprocessor. What it adds to a
guild is added in your name, exactly as if you had typed it.

Kaperkunde does not train anything on your cards, your training or your searches, and no
assistant changes that.

## What you add is the guild's, and what stays yours

You keep every right you have in the cards and pictures you make. What changes when you add
one to a guild is that the guild gets to use it: to show it, to hold it in every member's
stack, to vote on it and to improve its wording. A card nobody else has voted up is still yours
to delete. Once others have taken it up it stays with the guild, under your public name, or
under "a former member" once your account is gone. The
[terms of use](terms.md#2-what-you-add-belongs-to-the-guild) set this out in full. Your
card-by-card training is never anybody's but yours.

## Taking it with you, or taking it down

In **Settings** you can **download your data** as a machine-readable file (your account and
settings, stacks and progress, review history and sessions, the cards and edits you wrote,
your pictures and banners and banner votes, votes, reports, hidden cards, duplicate log, feats,
referrals, and the names and permissions of your connected apps) and **delete your account**, both at once and by yourself. If you cannot use Settings, email
**privacy@kaperkun.de** and we will do either for you; you hear back within 30 days, and
normally a great deal sooner than that. This is how Kaperkunde's company-wide export promise is
kept here: see [§4.1 of the Privacy Policy](../privacy.md#41-what-we-do-instead).

Deleting your account removes it, your settings, your sessions, your connected assistants, your
votes (any that were counted are taken back) and your entire training history. Cards that
other members have taken up stay with their guilds, credited to "a former member"; a card of
yours that nobody else has studied or voted on goes with you. "Studied or voted on" means
another member's progress, review or vote, or an edit by another member. A guild you founded
stays without a founder, and is erased only if nothing of anyone else remains in it. Banners
nobody else backs, and uploads no surviving card uses, are erased; banners others back and
uploads on surviving cards stay, with your name taken off. Referrals you made stay, credited to
no one. Deleted data may remain in backups until those expire or are overwritten.
