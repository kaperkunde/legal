AJ has terms of its own and **no privacy notice or subprocessor list of its own**: what it holds
and who it relies on are sections of the company-wide Privacy Policy, so the company details,
rights and transfers are written once. Only what is AJ's alone — the AI DJ and its voice, the
host and the guests, the music services, AJ tokens — is in this folder.

AJ is in alpha. There is **no acceptance gate**: creating an account is what accepts the terms
for a host, and joining a party is what accepts them for a guest. The sign-up form and the
guest's join screen both say so and link here.

### Why AJ has terms at all

- **The AI Act** (Article 50) asks that people know when they are talking to an AI and when a
  voice is synthetic. §1 says so, and the app says so where guests join.
- **The music is not ours.** AJ plays from the host's own Apple Music subscription or from
  YouTube, under their terms, and neither licenses public performance. §4 puts that on the
  record, and links the YouTube Terms of Service and Google Privacy Policy as the YouTube API
  Services Terms require.
- **Guests write in a shared chat and can ask the DJ to speak**, which makes AJ a hosting
  service under the EU Digital Services Act: the restrictions on what may be said, how reports
  are handled and a point of contact have to be in its terms (§5, §6).
- **Hosts buy AJ tokens.** §7 says what a token is and how unused bought tokens are refunded.
- Dutch law only lets the "offered as it is" limit of liability bind when it is in terms made
  available before the account is made.

## How these are published

AJ reads this repository through `@kaperkunde/legal-docs` and `@kaperkunde/legal`: `/terms` on
the site is [terms.md](terms.md), and `/privacy` is the company-wide
[Privacy Policy](../privacy.md), whose §2.5 and §5.4 are AJ's. A change here reaches the site
when the package version AJ depends on moves, which Renovate brings in as a patch.
