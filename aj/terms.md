AJ is an AI DJ for parties. A host opens a party, the screen that plays the music becomes the
deck, and guests join from their phones with a code or a QR code to vote, chat and ask for
songs. An AI model picks and orders the music, answers in the chat and, if the host lets it,
speaks between songs in a synthetic voice.

AJ is unfinished software in alpha. These are the terms of using it. Creating an account is
what accepts them for a host, and joining a party is what accepts them for a guest; both forms
say so and link here.

What AJ holds about you, and why, is in the [Kaperkunde Privacy Policy](../privacy.md#25-aj),
which has a section on AJ.

---

## 1. You are talking to an AI

The DJ is not a person. It is an AI model, run for AJ through Kaperkunde's Token Center, that
reads the room — what has played, the votes, the chat and the host's instructions — and decides
what to queue and what to say. Everything it writes in the chat, every reason it gives for a
song and every line it speaks is generated by that model.

**The voice is synthetic.** When AJ speaks between songs, the words are written by the model and
turned into speech by a text-to-speech model. It is not a recording of a real person, and it
does not imitate one.

An AI gets things wrong. It may state a fact about a song or an artist that is not true, mishear
what somebody asked for, or pick something that does not suit the moment. Treat what it says as
a DJ's patter, not as information to rely on. The host can always skip a song, change the queue,
pause the DJ or turn its voice off.

## 2. Who may use AJ

**Hosts** need an account. You need to be 16 or over, as
[§11 of the Privacy Policy](../privacy.md#11-children) sets out for all Kaperkunde services, and
18 or over (or a parent or guardian's agreement) to buy AJ tokens. During the alpha a new
account waits on a list until it is admitted by an invite code, by the admin, or by having been
a guest at a party. One account per person; do not share your password.

**Guests** do not need an account. A guest gives a name for the party and nothing else, and the
device they join from remembers them for next time. Guests need to be 16 or over too. A host who
invites younger guests is responsible for whether that is appropriate.

## 3. The host's party

A party is the host's. The host decides who gets the join code, what the DJ is told, which
artists and genres are off limits, whether explicit songs may play, and whether the DJ may
speak. The host can remove a guest at any time, and is responsible for the party: for who is
invited, for what is shown on the screen, and for keeping it to these terms.

Anything a host connects to AJ — an AI assistant allowed over OAuth to act as the DJ, a TV
signed in by QR code — acts under the host's account, and what it does is the host's doing, as
much as if they had typed it. A connection can be taken back at any time.

## 4. The music is not ours

AJ does not provide music and holds no licence to any of it. It chooses songs and tells the
deck what to play; the music itself comes from a service the host connects, on the device the
host uses as the deck, under that service's terms:

- **Apple Music**, through the host's own Apple Music subscription, under Apple's
  [Media Services Terms and Conditions](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/).
- **YouTube**, through YouTube's embedded player, under the
  [YouTube Terms of Service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms). AJ finds videos with the YouTube
  API Services, and by using AJ with YouTube you agree to be bound by YouTube's terms. YouTube's
  player is shown as YouTube provides it, advertisements included; AJ does not remove, block or
  alter them. What Google does with data from its player is in the
  [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

Those services license their music for personal, non-commercial use. **Playing it to the public
is not covered** — in a bar, a shop, a club, a gym, a business event, or anywhere people pay to
get in. Doing that needs a licence from the music rights organisations (in the Netherlands,
Buma/Stemra and Sena) and the music service's permission, and that is the host's responsibility,
not Kaperkunde's. During the alpha AJ is meant for private parties only.

To help the DJ choose, AJ looks songs up in public music databases — MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz,
Deezer, LRCLIB and Genius — for facts such as tempo, year and similar artists, and sometimes
for a song's lyrics, so the DJ can tell what a song is about. Lyrics are read by the DJ only:
AJ does not show them, and the DJ is told never to repeat them. Song facts from MusicBrainz and
ListenBrainz are made available by the MetaBrainz Foundation.

Song titles, artists and artwork shown in AJ belong to their owners and are shown to identify
what is playing.

## 5. What is not acceptable

For hosts and guests alike, in the chat, in a party's name, in instructions to the DJ, and in
anything you ask the DJ to say out loud:

- Anything illegal, and anything sexual involving children.
- Harassing, threatening or demeaning anyone, or inciting hatred against people for who they
  are.
- Impersonating a real person, or getting the DJ to — in the chat or in its voice.
- Trying to make the DJ say slurs, abuse, or anything else on this list, or to make it ignore
  the host's rules. The DJ is told to refuse, and trying anyway is a breach of these terms.
- Sending other people's personal details, such as an address or phone number, through the
  chat or the DJ.
- Attacking the service: getting into parties or accounts that are not yours, probing for holes
  you have not been invited to probe, scraping, flooding the chat or the votes, or getting round
  the limits on what the DJ may spend.
- Using AJ to play music to the public without the licences §4 describes.

A host can remove a guest who does any of this. Kaperkunde can remove content, end a party, or
suspend or close an account that breaks these terms or the law, and no further than they call
for.

## 7. AJ tokens

The DJ's model calls and its voice cost money, and are paid for in AJ tokens. Tokens are held
for the host by Kaperkunde's Token Center (tokens.kaperkun.de), the service that runs the AI
models for Kaperkunde's apps, and every call the DJ makes on a host's party is taken from that
host's balance. Guests never pay for anything.

- A host may get free tokens during the alpha, as the tokens page shows. Free tokens may expire
  as that page says.
- A host can buy tokens on the tokens page. The price is shown before you pay, and the payment is
  taken by Stripe on Kaperkunde's behalf. Bought tokens do not expire while AJ runs.
- A host can instead point AJ at a model of their own, where the tokens page offers it; calls to
  it cost no tokens, and the host's arrangement with that model's provider is theirs.
- Tokens are a prepaid balance for AI use in Kaperkunde's apps. They are not money, cannot be
  transferred to another person, and have no cash value, except as below.
- **If you change your mind:** unused bought tokens are refunded on request within 14 days of
  the purchase, to the card that paid. Ask **support@kaperkun.de**. If AJ shuts down, unused
  bought tokens are refunded too.
- When a host's balance runs out, the DJ stops until there is more. The music the host has
  queued keeps playing.
