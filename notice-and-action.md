Some Kaperkunde services publish what their members put into them, or carry names and traffic for
them. For those, the EU Digital Services Act asks for a few things to be written down once and
plainly: who authorities and members can reach, how to report something, and what you are told
when we act. This page is that text. It is shared by every Kaperkunde product; each product's
terms say what that product can remove or restrict, and link here for the rest.

---

## 1. Who to contact

**Authorities** (courts, regulators, law enforcement, the Dutch Digital Services Coordinator) with
an order or a request about any Kaperkunde service write to **legal@kaperkun.de**, in English or
Dutch. That address is Kaperkunde's single point of contact under Article 11 of the Digital
Services Act.

**Members and visitors** who want to reach us about a service, or about something on it, write to
**support@kaperkun.de** (anything about the service) or **abuse@kaperkun.de** (content or conduct
that breaks the rules or the law), in English or Dutch. These are ordinary mailboxes read by a
person, so there is no form to fill in and no automated tool to get past (Article 12).

---

## 2. Reporting something

Send a report to **abuse@kaperkun.de**. A report that helps us act quickly says:

- **where it is**: the exact address (URL) of the card, guild, picture, banner, name or page;
- **why it is a problem**: what is wrong with it, and the law or the product's rules it breaks;
- **who is reporting**: your name and email address, so we can reply. You may leave these out
  only for a report about child sexual abuse material or similar offences, where the law lets
  you stay anonymous;
- **that you mean it**: a line saying you believe in good faith that the report is accurate and
  complete.

A report that is missing something is still read. We may ask you for what is missing.

We confirm that we have received it. A person looks at it, and we tell you what we decided, and
on what ground, once we have.

Copyright and other intellectual-property complaints go to the same address, and should say
which work is yours and why the thing reported copies it.

## 3. What you are told when we act

When we remove or restrict something on a Kaperkunde service (hide or delete a card, guild,
picture or banner, limit what an account can do, or suspend or close it), the member or owner
concerned gets a message that states, as Article 17 requires:

- what we did, and for how long if it is not permanent;
- the facts and circumstances we relied on, and whether a report prompted it (the reporter's
  identity is never passed on);
- the rule of the product's terms or the law it breaks;
- whether an automated tool played a part in the decision;
- how to disagree: reply to the message and a person looks again, and you may in any case go to
  a court or, if you are a consumer, to the body that settles consumer disputes in your country.

The reporter hears the outcome too. We may leave out what we are not allowed to say, or what
would put someone at risk. Where we suspect a crime that threatens somebody's life or safety, we
tell the authorities (Article 18).

Every such decision, with its reason, is recorded.
